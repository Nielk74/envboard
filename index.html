Design notes

  1. The board is the summary. Every environment sits under its stage, with one line per stage such as "3 need attention · 1 deploying". Once you scroll past it, a slim strip of the same chips stays pinned under the header.
  2. Green means ready. A healthy environment gets one calm green tick. Problems add a bar, a word and a stronger colour, so they still stand out. Deploying is blue and is not counted as a problem.
  3. A familiar frame. A dark application bar with breadcrumb and global search, a page title with underlined tabs for the filters, stage groups you can fold in the table, and a status bar with platform health and live counts. The structure comes from JetBrains IDEs and TeamCity-style build tools.
  4. Reasons are readable. In the table they wrap to two lines instead of cutting off the ticket number. A flag shows who set it and when. On the board, hovering or focusing a tile shows the reason.
  5. Late deployments show up. If the nightly (Sandbox, Preview) or weekly (Rehearsal, Pre-live) deploy did not run, the environment turns amber: "Not deployed since Thu". PRV-2 is an example.
  6. Versions read like a departures board. The Nimbus release is aligned monospace. The Beacon version appears under it only when the two differ, with a small amber triangle on the board tile.
  7. Clear ownership. "Assign to me" only appears on free environments. Taking one from someone asks first and can end at a set time. The star means pinned, and your own environments say "You".
  8. Flags need a reason. The flag button opens a small form with a required reason. Hatched stripes mark a manual flag and appear nowhere else.

Assumptions in the sample data

  • Nightly deploys finish by 09:00 on Sandbox and Preview. Rehearsal and Pre-live deploy weekly on Friday.
  • A stopped relay only shows in the reason. It does not change the status on its own.
  • A failed smoke-test run does not change the status either. It shows in grey next to an OK environment.

All names, versions, tickets and people are made up. Inspired by the look of JetBrains products and TeamCity; not affiliated with or endorsed by JetBrains.
